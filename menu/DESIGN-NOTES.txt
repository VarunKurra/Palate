PALATE MENU · DESIGN NOTES

The guest-facing features follow the supplied Palate Business Plan.pdf:
photo dishes, ingredient details, dietary preferences, suggestions and
swaps, pairings, specials, translation and Palate attribution. The earlier
Tapmenu reference informed the simple photo-and-name rows. This is a new
implementation, not a copy of that site’s source.

The mobile layout puts the restaurant, a compact special, search and diet
filters before the dishes. Categories remain visible while browsing; the
heading count is separated into a small outlined badge. Decorative rules,
category taglines and the larger introduction were removed after feedback.
The recommendation form uses one question and one line explaining its
purpose. The same design adapts to tablets and desktops with a category
sidebar and a right-side detail panel. Right-to-left languages mirror it.

Component and interaction references:
Radix Dialog: https://www.radix-ui.com/primitives/docs/components/dialog
Radix Toggle Group: https://www.radix-ui.com/primitives/docs/components/toggle-group
W3C modal dialog pattern: https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialog-modal/
W3C target sizing: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html
Nielsen Norman Group, visible navigation:
https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/

These references informed visible navigation, keyboard focus handling,
Escape-to-close, appropriate hit areas and readable controls. They do not
constitute a claim of independently certified accessibility compliance.

SAMPLE PHOTOGRAPHY
All eight dishes are sample images. The existing pasta, dumplings and garden
bowl images came from the Palate project. Five additional images were made
with the built-in image generation tool and optimized as WebP assets.
These images are illustrations, not photos of an actual restaurant’s food.

Shared generation prompt:
“Use case: photorealistic-natural. Asset type: square sample restaurant menu
photograph. [SUBJECT] Beautiful appetizing real food photography, 45-degree
tabletop angle, entire dish/glass centered with breathing room all around.
Light warm stone tabletop, softly blurred natural light, crisp true-to-life
food textures, rich but natural color, refined casual bistro. No text,
labels, hands, people, logos, watermark, collage or other dishes. Single image.”

Subjects used:
• assets/burrata.webp — burrata with heirloom tomatoes, basil, olive oil and
  two slices of grilled sourdough on a white plate.
• assets/chicken.webp — herb-roast chicken breast with crisp skin, baby
  potatoes, green beans and lemon jus on a white plate.
• assets/cake.webp — a slice of chocolate ganache cake with vanilla cream
  and raspberry on a white plate.
• assets/lemonade.webp — strawberry basil lemonade, pink with ice in a glass.
• assets/tea.webp — amber jasmine iced tea with lemon and ice in a glass.

TRANSLATION NOTES
80 language bundles are stored locally. Initial additional-language copy
was produced through Google Translate’s public translation endpoint at
build time, with contextual corrections and manual spot checks. No external
translation call runs in the customer’s browser. Completeness tests cover
all interface keys, item details, ingredients and savings placeholders.
Translation quality is not equivalent to native restaurant review; see the
README before replacing the fictional restaurant with real menu data.
